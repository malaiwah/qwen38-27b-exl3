## Why all-FP4 is faster AND smaller

Converting the 64 attention projections to FP4 removes the pathological
`_exl3_gemm` path entirely (per-call reconstruct + Hadamard fold, 4.72 ms CPU
per call). Side effects: weights shrink 16.19 -> 15.88 GiB (FP4 at ~4.5
effective bits beats K6 trellis at 6 bits for these matrices) and peak
activation drops 2.32 -> 1.96 GiB because the 120 MB FP16 reconstruct
temporaries are gone. That freed 0.67 GiB of KV headroom on top of full native
context.

## The blocking structure (measured, not assumed)

No *configuration* of existing kernels satisfies all six, because the three
weight formats have opposite strengths and the crossover is in the kernels:

| format | M=1 decode | M=2051 prefill | KLD |
|---|---|---|---|
| trellis W4A16 | **fastest** (0.02-0.05 ms/call) | slowest (needs reconstruct+fold) | **0.0027** |
| FP4 W4A4 | slowest (0.25 ms/call; tile_m=64 wastes 63/64 rows, quantizer pads M->128) | **fastest** (164.7 ms/chunk) | 0.0633 |
| FP6 W6A8 | fast (0.14 ms/call) | 1.8-2.1x slower than FP4 (283.9 ms/chunk) | 0.0107 |

- TG >= 190 needs trellis-speed decode on the **MLP** too (63% of weights), and
  keeping both MLP trellis (10.62 GiB) and MLP FP4 (~12 GiB) resident is
  impossible. So M-based dispatch cannot rescue it.
- Therefore TG needs **one format that is fast at both ends**. The identified
  fix is the 16-row skinny-M FP4 tile (b12x issue #234): FP4 decode currently
  runs a 64-row tile with 63 rows masked.
- KLD <= 0.012 at PP >= 7000 needs FP4-class *weight* streaming with better
  than 4-bit *activations* — i.e. W4A8 (b12x issue #232).

Both are CuTe DSL kernel projects in b12x, now the authorised next step
(build + publish upstream).
