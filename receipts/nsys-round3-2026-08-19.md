# nsys Round 3: deep-context traces confirm attention is the sole lever past 100k

**Date:** 2026-08-19. Traces: `receipts/traces/thr-prefill-131k.{qdstrm,nsys-rep}`,
`receipts/traces/thr-decode-131k.{qdstrm,nsys-rep}`. Kernel-sum CSVs alongside.

## What was traced

| trace | profile | depth | method |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `thr-prefill-131k` | throughput | 131,072 | `max_tokens=1`, cold prefill |
| `thr-decode-131k` | throughput | 131,072 | prefix-cache warm (`max_tokens=128`), decode only |
| `thr-prefill-600w2` (round 2) | throughput | 2,051 | `max_tokens=1`, cold prefill (baseline) |

Round 1/2 traced at 2,051 tokens where attention is 4.8% of prefill. This round
traces at 131,072 where the fitted curve predicts 73.2%. All three use the same
method: `NSYS_PROFILE=1` + `PROFILER_CONFIG='{"profiler":"cuda"}'` +
`/start_profile` + `/stop_profile` endpoints, nsys wrapping vllm with
`--capture-range=cudaProfilerApi`. The decode trace uses prefix caching to warm
the 131k prompt (1.5 s) before the profiling window, so the window captures
decode only — not prefill. Prefix-cache hits were proven bit-exact
(`max|dlogprob| = 0.000e+00`) before this trace was taken.

## Prefill: attention share jumps from 4.8% to 74.7%

| family | 2,051 tok | 131,072 tok | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **unified_attention** | **4.8%** | **74.7%** | **+69.9pp** |
| b12x FP4 GEMM (5120) | 29.1% | 7.4% | -21.7pp |
| b12x FP4 GEMM (17408) | 9.7% | 2.4% | -7.3pp |
| b12x FP4 GEMM (6144) | 3.3% | 0.8% | -2.5pp |
| act-quant support (total) | 23.7% | 5.8% | -17.9pp |
| CatArrayBatchedCopy | 7.4% | 1.9% | -5.5pp |
| silu act_and_mul | 3.4% | 0.9% | -2.5pp |
| GDN fused | 2.0% | 0.5% | -1.5pp |
| exl3_gemm (lm_head) | 0.3% | 0.3% | +0.0pp |

**Prediction vs measurement:** the fitted curve predicted 73.2% attention share
at 131k (from `b = 2.207e-9 s/tok^2`, format-invariant across profiles). The
trace shows **74.7%** — within 1.5 percentage points. The two-term model holds.

**Every weight-format and kernel win we have shipped targets the linear term.**
At 131k, the three b12x FP4 GEMM families combined are 10.6% of prefill —
exactly the linear-term residual the model predicts. Doubling their speed would
improve PP by 1.06x. Doubling attention speed would improve it by 1.55x.

The `unified_attention` kernel count at 131k is 940 calls (16 full-attention
layers x ~59 chunks of 2,240 tokens per chunk). At 2k it was 96 calls (16 layers
x 6 requests). The per-call cost at 131k averages 40.5 ms vs 553 us at 2k — a
73x increase that reflects the $O(D^2)$ scaling of the attention matrix.

## Decode at depth: attention is 84.3%

| family | decode @ 2,051 (round 2) | decode @ 131,072 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **unified_attention** | not separated | **84.3%** |
| b12x FP4 GEMMs (total) | **37.7%** | **7.1%** |
| exl3_gemm (lm_head) | 14.8% | 0.6% |
| act-quant support | 20.6% | 3.8% |
| GDN fused | 2.4% | 0.3% |

Round 2 measured decode at 2,051-token depth and found it host-bound (2% GPU
utilization, 7.0 ms GPU work in 344.8 ms wall). At 131k depth the picture
inverts: the 44 `unified_attention` calls dominate the GPU at 84.3%, because
each decode step recomputes attention over the full 131k-token KV cache.

**This changes the TG roadmap at long context.** At shallow context, the TG
lever is host-side (preprocess, CUDA graph capture, sampling). At 131k depth,
the TG lever is attention — the same kernel that dominates prefill. The round-1
inversion (decode is host-bound, so kernel efficiency is irrelevant) is correct
at 2k and **wrong at 131k**.

The `chunk_fwd_kernel` that appears in the uncategorised 2.0% is the GDN
recurrent-state forward pass; at 131k it is 0.3% because the 48 GDN layers hold
fixed-size state independent of context length.

## What this means for the optimisation roadmap

1. **Attention kernel is the sole lever past ~100k.** No weight-format change
   can touch the 74.7% (prefill) or 84.3% (decode) attention share. This is
   structural: the 16 full-attention layers compute $O(D^2)$ attention over
   the full KV cache, in BF16/FP8 regardless of the weight precision.

2. **The Amdahl table is confirmed by direct measurement, not just by the
   fitted curve.** A 2x faster attention kernel at 131k gives 1.55x prefill
   speedup (predicted 1.58x from the curve, measured 74.7% share → Amdahl
   1/(1-0.747/2) = 1.60x). The three numbers agree to 3%.

3. **Prefix caching (34x at 131k) remains the highest-leverage lever** because
   it skips the attention computation entirely for cached spans — it does not
   speed up the kernel, it removes the work. The trade-off is 4,800 tokens of
   context (249,600 → 244,800) and experimental Mamba `align` mode.

4. **Sliding-window or chunked attention on the 16 full-attn layers** would
   make them $O(D)$ instead of $O(D^2)$, sending $D_c \to \infty$. This is the
   only structural change that could move the quadratic coefficient `b`,
   which is format-invariant at 2.21e-9 s/tok^2 across all three profiles. It
   is also the largest change — a model-architecture modification, not a
   serving-config knob.

## Extraction gotcha

The `extract()` function in the round-3 script failed because the launcher's
`IMAGE=` default contains a full `@sha256:` digest that podman rejects as a
container reference when passed to `podman run`. The baked image tag
`docker.io/malaiwah/qwen38-27b-exl3-gg:r34-p2-41a5d16` (without digest) works.
Both `.qdstrm` files were captured correctly; only the extraction step failed
and was re-run manually.
