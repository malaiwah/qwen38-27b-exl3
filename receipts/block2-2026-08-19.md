## 2. The KLD is identical to all printed digits

| | mean | ci95 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| balanced, ANY_BITS **off** | 0.005672 | [0.005302, 0.006087] | 0.059908 |
| balanced, ANY_BITS **on** | **0.005672** | [0.005302, 0.006087] | **0.059908** |

Not "statistically indistinguishable" — *identical*, to every digit reported. This is
the expected result and it is worth stating why it is strong evidence rather than a
suspicious coincidence:

1. The selftest already proved the two routes agree numerically (cos ≥ 0.999996 across
   bits 4/5/6, prefill and decode rows).
2. The capture/replay pipeline was separately proven **bit-reproducible** (a repeat
   capture reproduced mean and p99 to 18 digits; run-to-run SD = 0).

Given (1) and (2), identical inputs through agreeing routes *must* produce identical
KLD. An identical number here is the pipeline working as characterised; a *differing*
number would have indicated a real fidelity change. The bit-reproducibility work paid
for itself by making this one-run comparison conclusive.

## 3. balanced under concurrency

| level | aggregate | per-user | MTP acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 95.4 | 95.4 | 0.299 |
| C4 | **275.3** | 68.8 | **0.380** |
| C8 | 268.9 | 33.6 | 0.336 |

Same shape as the other profiles: aggregate throughput saturates by C4, and MTP
acceptance *rises* under load (0.299 → 0.380) because larger batches give the draft
head more context to verify against. C8 is flat-to-slightly-down versus C4 — C4 is the
efficient operating point on this card. Note the C1 spread ([125.9, 82.1, 95.4]) is
much wider than C4/C8; single-stream timing is the noisy measurement, not the
concurrent one.
